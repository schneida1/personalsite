01 — Context

Valuable data, hard to see.

Every year, participants in the UN Global Compact report on how they're putting the Ten Principles into practice through the Communication on Progress (CoP). In 2025 that was 16,256 submissions from 157 countries.

That's one of the broadest pictures of corporate sustainability anywhere, but it's only useful if people can explore it: companies comparing themselves to peers, researchers looking for trends, and the public seeing what business is actually doing.

02 — What's in it

From the big picture to a single question.

  • Year-over-year overviewHeadline submission trends with explorable breakdowns by region, organization type, and sector.
  • Topic dashboardsGovernance, Human Rights & Labour, Environment, and Anti-Corruption, with question-level charts across 2023–2025.
  • One filter panel everywhereRegion, country, organization type, sector, and subsector, consistent on every page.
  • Compare modePut submission trends side by side instead of flipping between views.
  • Resources in contextThe CoP questionnaire, guidebook, and FAQ are one click from the data.
  • Built for a global audienceA language selector and a clear data disclaimer on every page.
03 — Choices that shaped it

Making a lot of data feel approachable.

  1. Start broad, then let people drill in.

    Overview numbers first, then topics, then individual questions, so first-time visitors aren't dropped into a wall of charts.

  2. Sensible defaults.

    The sector chart opens on the five largest sectors instead of every sector at once. Everything else is one selection away.

  3. Put context next to the numbers.

    Notes explain things that could be misread, like the 2023 dip and the fact that uploaded reports aren't included, right where the chart is.

  4. Ask how it's going, inside the product.

    A short in-product rating prompt gives the team a steady read on whether the tool is actually useful.

04 — My role

Product Owner, as part of a team.

As Product Owner for reporting at the UN Global Compact, I helped shape Insights alongside designers, engineers, and colleagues across the organization: defining what it needed to do, prioritizing what shipped, and using AI tools to prototype ideas quickly so we could react to something real before it went into development.
