01 · United Nations Global Compact

Better data, from the first keystroke.

Thousands of companies report their sustainability progress to the UN Global Compact every year. I use AI to build and ship features that make that data more trustworthy and the reporting less painful, then publish it so anyone can use it.

  • Caught at entryData-quality problems flagged while companies report, not weeks later by an analyst
  • Days → minutesLLM synthesis of reports, with analysts reviewing instead of reading
  • 16,256 reportsfrom 157 countries, turned into a public platform on Insights

Reporting was long and confusing, and bad data was only found after it was too late to fix cheaply.

Every year, participants complete the Communication on Progress (CoP), a detailed questionnaire on governance, human rights, labour, environment, and anti-corruption. Companies of every size, in dozens of languages, fill in hundreds of fields.

Mistakes like a total that doesn't add up, a wrong unit, or a "yes" with no evidence slipped through and were only caught later by analysts, if at all. That made the data harder to trust and the insights slower to publish.

  • Fix data quality where it's created. AI-assisted validation runs while companies fill in the questionnaire. Problems show up next to the field, with a plain-language reason and a one-click fix where possible. Trade-offMore friction during reporting, so checks are specific, explain themselves, and warnings never block submission.
  • AI drafts, people decide. LLMs read and synthesize reports; analysts review and sign off. Their time moves from reading to judging, and accuracy holds up.
  • Prototype the feature, not the document. I write requirements in plain language and use AI to generate working React prototypes from them, so stakeholders react to something real in days, not to a spec.
  • Close the loop with real usage. Set up engagement, task-completion, and adoption metrics, ran a global early-adopter program, and built feedback into the product itself.
  • AI-powered validation layer: rules that catch inconsistent, missing, or implausible answers during reporting.
  • LLM synthesis pipeline: turns report analysis that took days into minutes, at the same accuracy.
  • Insights: a public platform to explore CoP data year over year by region, sector, organization type, and the Ten Principles.
  • A roadmap driven by usage instead of a quarterly planning doc.

02 · Betawise

288 readings a day. One thing to work on.

I have Type 1 diabetes. My CGM takes a reading every five minutes, and I see an endocrinologist for about fifteen minutes a few times a year. Betawise is the coach in between that I wanted for myself.

Role
Founder · product & build
For
People with T1D & T2D
Data
Dexcom · Clarity · LibreView
Live
betawise.org ↗
  • 4,000 → 1two weeks of readings become one clear weekly focus
  • 0 raw readingssent to the AI. Analysis runs on your device; no account needed.
  • 3 clinical standardsTime in Range, Glycemia Risk Index, and GMI, computed to their published definitions

CGMs made glucose data free. Understanding it is still a privilege.

About 4,000 readings pile up every two weeks in apps that show a squiggly line and an average. The clinical reports are written for doctors. The person actually living with diabetes gets lots of data and no plan.

  • One weekly focus, not a dashboard. The pattern engine finds everything (overnight lows, dawn phenomenon, post-meal spikes, rebound highs) but asks you to work on one thing. Trade-offHides detail power users want, so the full metrics are one tap away.
  • Safety always ranks first. If lows exceed consensus limits, they become the focus, even when fixing highs would gain more time in range. Try "Night lows" in the demo.
  • AI explains the numbers; it never invents them. Every metric is deterministic, tested code built on published definitions. The AI coach only talks about the results.
  • Your readings never leave your device. Analysis runs in the browser. The coach receives a de-identified summary, with no raw readings, dates, or device IDs.
  • Guardrails that don't depend on the model. Dosing questions, emergencies, and crisis language are caught by fixed rules before any AI call, and get fixed, safe responses.
  • Bring your data: connect Dexcom or drop in a Clarity / LibreView export.
  • Clinical metrics: consensus targets, Glycemia Risk Index, and an Ambulatory Glucose Profile.
  • Pattern engine with a ranked weekly focus.
  • AI peer coach with structured weekly insights.
  • Clinic-ready report: one page to bring to your next appointment.
03 · Jarvis

The best input is the one you already use.

Every notes app asks you to open it, choose a folder, and fill in a form. But the fastest thing anyone does on a phone is send a text. Jarvis is a notes app you use by texting yourself.

Role
Designer & builder
Platform
iPhone · web sync
Stack
SwiftUI · SwiftData · App Intents
Status
In daily personal use
  • 0 formsTags, reminders, to-dos, and calendar events come from the text itself
  • 5 ways inSiri, Action button, Shortcuts, iMessage to yourself, or its own phone number
  • 4 views, 1 storeFeed, Board, To-do, and Calendar always agree. Works offline.

Every tap between a thought and saving it loses some thoughts.

Ideas show up at bad times: walking, in a meeting, half-asleep. Anything that does get saved then has to be organized later, which mostly never happens.

  • Syntax instead of forms. #tags, "remind me…", "- sub-items", and #event are parsed from plain text. The message itself sets the structure. Trade-offHard to discover, so the app gives examples and the syntax is forgiving ("at 9am", "tomorrow 3pm", "in 30 minutes").
  • Capture from anywhere. Siri, the Action button, Shortcuts, a Messages automation, and a dedicated phone number via Twilio and a Cloudflare Worker.
  • Offline-first, sync second. The phone keeps a full copy and syncs with the web in the background. If a note is edited in two places, the latest edit wins.
  • Glanceable, not openable. Lock-screen widgets and a Live Activity show today's reminders, so most of the time you never open the app.
  • Natural-language reminders as local notifications
  • #event notes added straight to Apple Calendar
  • Permanent IDs (N-0024) so notes can be referenced
  • Lock screen, Dynamic Island, Siri, and Shortcuts support
  • Web sync and CSV export
How I work

Four rules I keep coming back to.

01

Catch problems where they start.

The cheapest moment to fix bad data, or a bad decision, is the moment it's made. Design feedback for that moment.

In practice: UN validation at entry; Betawise's safety checks before any AI call.

02

Show less, so people act more.

A dashboard with twelve numbers gets admired and then ignored. One clear next step gets done.

In practice: Betawise's single weekly focus, with the full detail one tap away.

03

AI for the reading, people for the judgment.

Models are great at turning lots of text into something useful, but they shouldn't make the final call where it matters. I design that boundary on purpose.

In practice: UN analysts sign off on AI synthesis; Betawise's math is deterministic.

04

Meet people at the habit they already have.

New behavior is expensive. I'd rather fit into an existing habit than teach a new one.

In practice: Jarvis works over text messages; Betawise reads the CGM export you already have.
