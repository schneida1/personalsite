Understanding TIR

What is Time
In Range?

Time In Range (TIR) is the percentage of time a person with diabetes spends with blood glucose between 70–180 mg/dL. Unlike A1C — a blunt 90-day average — TIR reveals the full picture: dangerous highs, scary lows, and the hours spent in a healthy zone. It's one of the most actionable metrics in modern diabetes care.

Why It Matters

The data behind
the mission

22%
Complication Risk Reduction

Every 5% increase in Time In Range is associated with a 22% reduction in the risk of diabetic retinopathy — one of the leading causes of blindness in working-age adults.

55%
Average TIR Today

The average person with diabetes achieves only 55% time in range — 15 points below the recommended target, representing nearly 4 hours daily spent outside the safe zone.

500M+
People Living With Diabetes

Over 500 million people worldwide live with diabetes. Better TIR tools could meaningfully reduce the $966B annual global health expenditure and improve quality of life at scale.

My Story

A diagnosis that
changed everything

I was diagnosed with Type 1 Diabetes, and from day one I was handed a CGM and told to "stay in range." What nobody explained was how hard that actually is — or how to make sense of the torrent of data a continuous glucose monitor produces every five minutes, around the clock.

As a product person, my instinct was to look for the gap between the data that existed and the understanding that was missing. That gap was enormous. Patients have access to incredibly rich, real-time health data — and almost no intuitive tools to act on it.

The Time In Range Project is my personal response to that problem. It's where my professional skills in product management, AI, and data meet a cause that is deeply, personally real. Every percentage point of TIR improvement isn't an abstract metric — it's time spent feeling well, avoiding complications, and living fully.

My goal is to build tools and awareness that make Time In Range understandable, motivating, and achievable — for myself and for every T1D patient navigating this every day.

The Problem

T1D patients generate enormous amounts of glucose data. Most tools surface numbers without context, pattern recognition, or actionable guidance.

The Mission

Make Time In Range visible, understandable, and motivating — using product design and AI to turn raw CGM data into real behavior change.

Why It's Personal

Living with T1D means this isn't an abstract challenge. Every improvement in TIR is felt — in energy, mood, sleep, and long-term health. That's what drives this work.

Approach

The product methodology

01
Understand the User

Deep research into the daily lived experience of T1D patients — the mental load of glucose management, the emotional weight of out-of-range readings, and the moments where better data visualization could shift behavior. User interviews with patients, parents, and endocrinologists.

User Research
02
Map the Data Landscape

CGMs like Dexcom and Abbott FreeStyle Libre generate rich time-series glucose data. The project maps what data is available, how it's currently surfaced, and where the biggest gaps in interpretation and action exist for end users.

Data Architecture
03
Build for Behavior Change

Using product design principles, the project focuses on surfacing TIR in motivating, non-clinical ways — progress visualization, pattern recognition (what behaviors correlate with better range?), and gentle nudges rooted in behavioral science.

Product Design · UX
04
Leverage AI for Insight

Applying the same LLM and analytics techniques used at the UN Global Compact to the T1D space — natural language summaries of glucose trends, anomaly detection, and proactive recommendations based on real-time and historical patterns.

LLM · AI Analytics
05
Advocacy & Awareness

Sharing the work openly — writing, speaking, and connecting with the broader T1D community to raise awareness about Time In Range and the tools that can help. The more people understand TIR, the more they can advocate for better care and better technology.

Advocacy · Community

Advocacy

Why this work
matters to me

T1D Diagnosis · Personal Mission

Type 1 Diabetes is a 24/7 condition. There's no off switch, no vacation from glucose management, no day where it doesn't factor into every meal, every workout, every night of sleep. Living with T1D gave me an intimate understanding of what patients actually need from health technology — and how far most tools fall short.

Advocacy, for me, isn't about awareness campaigns in the abstract. It's about using every skill I have — product management, AI, data design — to make life measurably better for people navigating what I navigate every day.

The Time In Range Project is that work made concrete. It's mission-driven not because a job description said so, but because it's personal.

The Daily Reality

T1D means checking glucose levels constantly, adjusting insulin, and reacting to readings that can shift dramatically within minutes. Better tools reduce that cognitive burden significantly.

The Opportunity

CGMs generate more health data per day than most people see in a year of doctor visits. The opportunity to turn that data into meaningful, real-time guidance is enormous — and largely untapped.

The Intersection

Product management, AI, behavioral design, and lived patient experience rarely meet in one person. That's the intersection the Time In Range Project is built on.
